       Observed cluster tests of the conservative stock sector


                 Research calculation for the Entropic Scalar EFT project

                                   23 October 2026



                                         Abstract

         Public radial profiles for twelve X-COP clusters, five matching weak-lensing mass estimates and
      a released Bullet Cluster lens reconstruction provide the first resolved observational targets for the
      conservative stock dynamics. The existing surviving cohort allocation is held fixed. A restricted model
       in which stock follows the present baryons is insufficient across these targets. A2142 is close to its
     mass budget; A1795 requires substantial relative segregation if its independent lensing mass is adopted.
     The Bullet reconstruction supplies simultaneous aperture-mass, gas-separation and galaxy-alignment
       targets. These calculations constrain the required outcome of transport. They do not demonstrate
      that the proposed interaction produces it.


1  The tested statement


The preceding encounter calculation evolved gravitating stock, stellar tracers and baryonic gas with
reciprocal impulses and a finite-work contact. Its initial profiles and coupling parameters were specified.
Here the gravitating profiles come from observations, and their associated assumptions remain explicit.

We retain the project’s saturated surviving allocation

It is a stock-to-original-cohort-baryon ratio. For a restricted comparison, impose the stronger assumption
that stock and present baryons occupy each aperture in that ratio:

Equation (2) is a testable shortcut, not a necessary consequence of the theory. A gravitating target

For a single conserved cohort with aperture retentions rb and rc, η = rb/rc. In a mixed aperture it is
an effective inventory diagnostic: imported material must be tracked separately. Values above one can
reflect preferential stock loss or imported baryons. Neither sign of η −1 alone establishes a violation of
the whole-cohort inventory.

The normalization is unchanged between clusters. The full-release convention is itself a conditional input;
it is not evidence that each observed cluster has reached the same release state. No per-cluster coupling,
stock amplitude or acceleration scale is fitted here.

2  Resolved X-COP profiles


We downloaded the complete primary X-COP archive and read its FITS tables for all twelve clusters [1,
2, 3]. Seven have released stellar profiles; the other five receive gas-only lower-bound diagnostics. No
stellar mass is invented for those five. The comparison uses the released hydrostatic masses without a
correction that enforces the cosmic baryon fraction.





Solid blue: equation (2) divided by the NFW hydrostatic mass. Dashed orange: the same baryons divided by
the forward hydrostatic reconstruction. Blue shading spans zero to the measured projected stellar mass, for the
measured stellar component only; it is not a confidence band. Dotted blue doubles the adopted deprojected stellar
contribution. This is a sensitivity scenario for stellar-population and diffuse-light uncertainty, not a hard upper
bound.

All principal radial tests start at 0.1R500 and stop within the available gas, mass and stellar profiles,
with a maximum of 1.5R500. The measured shapes generally require more centrally concentrated stock
than a local rescaling of the gas and stars. Agreement near one outer aperture does not reproduce the
inner profile. Correlated radial samples are not summed into a fictitious independent-bin χ2.

3  Mass inventory and reconstruction sensitivity


The stellar FITS profiles are projected cumulative masses. Ghizzardi et al. apply a factor 0.75 at
R500 to obtain a spherical mass and exclude intracluster light [4]. We preserve that distinction. Our
radial extension uses the geometric sphere-to-cylinder ratio of a spherical generalized NFW distribution
with inner slope 1.64, choosing its scale once so that D(R500) = 0.75. This gives R500/rs = 1.28214.
Multiplying the measured profile by this radius-dependent factor is an approximation, not a recovered
three-dimensional stellar distribution. The reported geometric and doubled-star sensitivities bracket
alternatives for this measured component.

All entries are evaluated at the released hydrostatic R500. The source tables, interpolation results and
full precision are saved alongside this note.

The nominal NFW reconstruction requires positive cumulative stock and positive stock in each of fifteen
finite radial shells for every cluster tested. This is a conditional consistency result. The NFW mass shape
was supplied by the observational reconstruction, and positive shell masses do not prove a nonnegative
distribution function, stable equilibrium or formation history.

The less constrained forward reconstruction for A644 gives four negative residual shell masses over the
tested range. We retain this failure.  It prevents a blanket claim that arbitrary released hydrostatic
profiles admit a positive stock density, and points to sensitivity to the mass reconstruction and hydrostatic
assumptions.





The required relative retention changes with radius. These curves are inferred targets; selecting them as an
interaction’s equilibrium would not constitute a prediction.

4  Five independent weak-lensing comparisons


We matched A1795, A2029, A2142, A85 and ZW1215 to the public MENeaCS results [5]. The lensing
masses do not use the X-ray hydrostatic equilibrium equation.  They retain their authors’ NFW
concentration prescription and simulation calibration. The aperture and NFW methods use the same
shear observations and are not counted as independent datasets.

The coextensive range changes the adopted stellar mass from one to two times its nominal value. It is
not a statistical interval.





A1795 is the most useful stress test in this overlap sample: its two lensing estimators agree closely and
require roughly half as much retained baryonic mass per unit retained stock as a coextensive cohort. Its
nominal mass difference is 2.43 times the quoted NFW lensing error alone (2.30 with doubled stars).
These ratios are descriptive residuals, not a combined exclusion significance. A2029 also requires relative
segregation. ZW1215 is sensitive to the lensing estimator, so its hydrostatic deficit cannot be promoted
to an equally robust lensing discrepancy.

The released subcluster halo centre is 3.92 kpc from BCG3, reproducing the published roughly 4 kpc
alignment. The supplied gas-density map has its local peak about 169 kpc from BCG3; this is a
native-pixel diagnostic with an 8.68 kpc gas pixel, not an X-ray brightness-peak measurement or a fitted
offset posterior. Aperture integration changes by at most 0.214% when pixel sampling is coarsened.
Observational and model uncertainties are larger.

These are measurements of an existing lens reconstruction. No simulated map has been optimized
against it. The previous encounter’s 24–30 kpc force softening, much broader map smoothing, different
mass ratio and specified Plummer profiles cannot establish agreement at the observed few-kiloparsec
alignment.

6  What the effective dynamics must now accomplish


Three complementary targets emerge. A2142 provides a useful normalization and equilibrium benchmark.
A1795 demands a quantitative assembly and transport explanation while retaining the observed gas
profile. The Bullet demands separated gas and gravitating concentrations together with close halo–
galaxy alignment. A model should meet these requirements using the same constitutive coupling, with
environmental state changes computed from the work and depletion variables.

The useful improvement is a set of numerical obligations. A nonnegative stock inventory can be written
for the nominal NFW targets, and the previous conservative experiment supplies a candidate transport
mechanism. Connecting them requires a selected phase-space distribution and coupling law that survive
an isolated-cluster control and then the merger. The strongest previously tested contact changed the
isolated halo appreciably, so increasing its rate is not an established solution to A1795’s retention target.
Depletion can suppress the interaction, but assigning a favorable depleted state to each cluster would
introduce a hidden fit unless its history is computed.

A next forward calculation should fix gas and stellar inputs, specify the initial cohort and formation
history, and derive the stock evolution under the common potential. Lensing profiles, projected gas
morphology and galaxy offsets then provide the comparison. Fitting the observed total mass first and
calling the recovered stock a prediction would not perform this test. A contact-rate bound also requires
this forward calculation; a particle-dark-matter self-interaction limit cannot simply be relabeled as the
rate of the present stock–baryon process.


7  Scope and reproducibility

The analysis adopts the observational H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1, Ωm = 0.3 distance convention. Small
redshift differences between the two catalogues are retained in the source record; the largest distance
mismatch is about 2% for ZW1215. We use the X-COP redshift for radius conversion and do not
claim a recalibration of the original shear-source efficiencies. Stellar deprojection, missing diffuse light,
hydrostatic departures, triaxiality, correlated profile errors and lens-model systematics remain relevant.
No joint likelihood or new theorem of microphysical uniqueness is claimed.

The project directory contains the pre-calculation protocol; the original X-COP archive; 115 expanded
FITS tables; the primary lensing paper and transcribed five-row table; the Bullet maps, catalogues and
parameter files; acquisition URLs and checksums; twelve radial result tables; and separate radial and
Bullet summaries. An oversized whole-folder Bullet download was stopped after the required small input
files were recovered and individually CRC-checked. Its partial archive is preserved, and the complete
convergence map was retrieved directly with a recorded checksum.

The calculation scripts are radial_analysis.py and bullet_analysis.py. validate.py checks in-
dependent critical-density normalization, overdensity roots, cohort normalization, stellar projection
convention, world-coordinate round trips, map subtraction, aperture convergence and source hashes. All
fifteen numerical/data-handling checks pass. The A644 forward-profile failure is explicitly retained as an
analysis result. These checks validate the reduction, not the theory. The four publication papers and
preceding simulation outputs are unchanged.
