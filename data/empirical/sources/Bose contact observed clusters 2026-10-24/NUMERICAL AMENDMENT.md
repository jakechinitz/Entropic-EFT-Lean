# Numerical refinement and reservoir sensitivity

The initial 0.5 Myr runs of the archived implementation showed a mechanical energy residual that was not small compared with the 15,000 (km/s)^2 available-work reserve. For A1795 at three proposals/orbit its maximum was 0.00925 times M_stock V500^2; reducing the timestep to 0.1 Myr reduced it only to approximately 0.00265. These screening outputs are retained, but are not used for final quantitative claims about a finite reservoir.

The archived code computes gravity from grid-interpolated cumulative particle counts but diagnoses self-energy with exact sorted shells. At finite particle number these are different force/energy discretizations. The refined check derives the particle force from precisely that sorted-shell energy, excludes each particle's own shell, and differentiates precisely the same fixed baryon potential used in the energy account. This is a conservative discretization of the same spherical continuum force. The archived Bose derivative, orbit averaging, nearest-neighbour contacts, central impulses, proposed rates, work register and acceptance function remain unchanged. The contact-orbit potential is still the archived finite radial-grid approximation.

The refined suite uses 0.1 Myr and 2,000 particles for every cluster and rate. For A1795 and ZW1215 it also checks 8,000 particles with another seed, paired with their no-contact controls. Those two changes together test robustness but cannot separately measure seed variance and resolution convergence. A larger common reserve, 150,000 (km/s)^2, is tested for these two clusters because the initial pilot exhausted the smaller reserve. Neither reserve is claimed to have been selected by the theory; the saved galaxy models themselves use different budgets.

An initial run of the new conservative adapter failed because a local momentum diagnostic reused the name of the baryon potential captured by the force function. This was an adapter coding error, not a physical result. Its directory is retained (`force_refinement`); `force_refinement_v2` and all `refined` runs use distinct names. The force is checked against finite differences of its energy before accepting the corrected run.

These checks refine a conditional survival experiment. They do not provide a formation likelihood, a joint gas/lensing fit, or a thermal embedding of the contact functional.

The unsoftened conservative run subsequently encountered an unresolved central passage: its energy error jumped discontinuously near 4 Gyr. Those outputs are also screening-only. The unsoftened refined suite was stopped, preserving all partial results. The accepted regularization replaces the stock shell energy denominator r by sqrt(r^2+epsilon^2), differentiates it exactly, and uses the corresponding softened monopole potential for the orbit calculations. Epsilon is 5 kpc, at most 0.5% of R500, and the force change at the smallest compared radius (0.1 R500) is below 0.4%. A finer particle/seed check also reduces epsilon to 2.5 kpc. These are numerical resolution choices, not cluster fitting parameters. Baryon completion and Eddington initialization remain the unsoftened ones; the resolved-region effect is small and all results are paired with the same no-contact initialization.

The final suite uses this conservative regularization and 0.1 Myr. An A1795 check also uses 0.05 Myr. A cache of the position-only acceleration removes redundant sorting between adjacent kicks; a direct comparison of old and cached kicks on the same 2,000-particle state gives exactly zero maximum velocity difference. Each run preserves its executed source. This speed change does not change the force or integration order.
