# Cross-sector work allocation

During the numerical checks, inspection of the three saved galaxy allocations showed that the different dimensional budgets are almost exactly a shared dimensionless rule: D/Vf^2 = 0.447 (Milky Way), 0.452 (dwarf), and 0.450 (massive spiral), where Vf^2 = sqrt(G M_b,total a0). Thus the protocol's fixed 15,000 (km/s)^2 cluster allowance is a low-work sensitivity case, not a faithful extrapolation of the galaxy work scaling.

The additional shared-scaling suite applies D = 0.45 sqrt(G M_b,total a0) to all seven clusters, at both one and three attempts per orbit, with exactly the same 2,000-particle starts, timestep, conservative force treatment, and no-contact controls. No mass-specific or cluster-specific coefficient is fitted. M_b,total is the asymptotic mass of the explicitly completed baryon profile, not a separately measured all-space cluster baryon mass. The allocation file also records the smaller supply obtained using only measured baryons inside R500.

The common coefficient is inferred from the saved simulation choices, not selected by Lean or the microscopic theory. This extension is necessary to distinguish protection by a deliberately small work allowance from protection by the prescription actually shared by the saved galaxy runs. Earlier results and comments about the small budget remain valid only for that conditional case.

Before the initially planned intermediate-budget and 8,000-particle low-budget jobs started, those queued checks were superseded. The already-running final low-budget/control jobs were allowed to finish. Resolution effort was redirected to the shared dimensionless allocation: two paired no-contact/contact checks at 4,000 particles, another seed and 2.5 kpc smoothing (A1795 and ZW1215), and an A1795 shared-allocation check at 0.05 Myr. The final accepted set is 40 runs: 21 restricted-budget/control, 14 shared-allocation and five checks. This change followed discovery of the common galaxy normalization, not selection on a cluster outcome. Existing protocol and queue manifests remain preserved as the audit trail.
