1 The empirical picture
A galaxy’s gravitational field may depend on how its matter assembled and what happened to it
afterward. In the model reviewed here, baryons support a persistent stock of committed substrate
capacity. That stock carries gravitating mass and inertia, survives a change in the surrounding
field, and can be stripped by tides. A related cosmological process transfers a small share of
committed capacity into a homogeneous vacuum component when the supporting baryonic source
loses its recoverable progenitor identity. These proposals connect galaxy dynamics with the
expansion history.
Galaxy, cosmological, and cluster results test different parts of this account. They do not have
equal evidential status. The LMC comparison begins with a short analytic mass calculation.
Crater II requires a numerical history. The redshift test compares a calculated function with a
published fitted relation. The cosmological result joins a physical assembly-and-mixing history to
the CMB, baryon acoustic oscillations, and supernova distances.

3.6 Catalogue tests and the direction of a relaxation correction
The common cosmology was propagated to 73 KMOS3D, 21 KGES, and 169 KROSS objects at
three radii. At the KMOS3D outer radius, the log-acceleration residual slope is −0.245 dex per

log-velocity residual −0.087 dex. The reference z = 2.3 example gives acceleration boosts about
2.02 versus 1.39 for the compared evolving and fixed scales. These are catalogue central-value
diagnostics with specified baryon force models; they do not constitute raw-cube likelihoods. The
112-point reference projection remains distinct from a regression on the actual 79-object MUSE
selection. The common cosmology has not removed the high-redshift residual trend. [U1, S01]
Passive relaxation cannot automatically supply a reduction. If ˙n = Γ(q −n) with Γ > 0, ˙q ≤0,
and initially n ≥q, then n remains above the target. At fixed baryons a lag behind declining H
therefore has the wrong sign to suppress an initially equilibrated response. Underoccupied assembly

the tested adiabatic cosmological mass loss gives an inferred scale ratio 0.9183 from z = 2.3,
compared with H0/H(2.3) = 0.2972. These constraints identify the contact and preparation
history as physical requirements. [D1]

correlations, and gravitational coupling remain unspecified, so this is a contact-model constraint.

5.8 What the Crater II result changes
The successful stock trajectories show that a diffuse cold remnant can emerge from a specified

9.2 Which conventional assumptions are challenged
For the LMC, the comparison challenges the applicability of an instantaneous external-field
suppression to a previously established gravitating stock. A retained distribution continues to
contribute after the ambient field changes. The tested QUMOND prescriptions and the stock
model make different mass predictions with the same stated baryons. Joint stream and reflex
modeling can test that distinction more directly than comparing scalar enclosed-mass summaries.
For Crater II, the issue is the central structure and stripping response of the excess gravitating
mass. The tested capacity profile loses enough support to produce a cold, extended remnant under
the adopted orbit and initial conditions. Standard-concentration NFW halos under the tested
abundance prior perform poorly in that comparison. Low-concentration and cored dark-matter
alternatives require their own matched tests. Assembly history already matters in conventional
dark-matter dynamics; history dependence alone cannot distinguish the two frameworks.
For the acceleration scale, the proposed change is a cosmological normalization a0 ∝H. Its observed
value at one epoch would cease to be an independent constant. The published intermediate-
redshift relation is consistent with the fixed reference curve under the stated projection. A direct
galaxy-level likelihood would test both its curvature and its scatter.
For dark energy, the proposed change is a vacuum density generated by irreversible source-identity
loss. The microscopic conversion and formation abundance restrict a model that still fits the
CMB and distance data. The remaining release normalization must be obtained independently
before the observed vacuum abundance can be called a prediction of the microstructure alone.
The current evidence supports these mechanisms as testable alternatives; it does not establish
the elimination of all dark-matter models, all MOND formulations, or the absolute cosmological
constant problem.
9.3 A joint test of the same construction
The galactic comparisons use the reference H0 = 67.4 km s−1 Mpc−1, while the matched linear-
lensing release fit gives 68.9389. If horizon contact is evaluated at the latter value, the local scale
rises by about 2.3%. A joint fit must propagate the fitted H(z) into galaxy assembly and the
acceleration relation, including any finite relaxation time of an existing stock. The reference-curve
agreement and the cosmological best fit are presently separate calculations.
The same requirement applies to the Milky Way potential. The available moving-LMC calculations
use a Galactic mass within 50 kpc about 17% above the Brooks et al. inference in the stock model,
and about 36% above it in the NFW model. A joint Milky Way–LMC fit would alter the satellite
pericentres and the stripping histories. Those changes should be propagated to both Crater II
and Antlia 2 before assigning a combined evidence ratio. [G4][5]
